# PyPKA PB profile

The matching five states average **62.62 s PB on one pinned core** in the earlier uninstrumented PyPKA benchmark, versus **8.78 s with 12 workers** and jaxpb OPT4's **1377.34 s at one CPU / 351.20 s at 12 CPUs**. The new single-core, log-instrumented run averaged 79.98 s PB; instrumentation adds overhead. All five reproduced campaign MC energies bitwise and pKa values exactly. Peak sampled process-tree RSS was 480 MiB (shared pages can be counted twice).

PyPKA 2.10.0 calls delphi4py 1.3.0's compiled Fortran DelPhi library in `envs/pypka`, not an external executable. Per tautomer it solves a whole-protein coarse grid (1 Å spacing, screened Coulomb boundary), a focused grid (0.25 Å, interpolated coarse boundary), and a model compound (0.25 Å, Coulomb boundary). Each is 81³, predominantly single precision. Tautomer coordinates/radii are prepared through the shared wrapper; maps and solves are invoked anew for each call. Logs describe atom-pair surface construction, accessible-point indexing, re-entrant surface and dielectric/ion maps. Corrected reaction-field and Coulomb energies and interpolated atom potentials feed pKint and interactions.

| Stage | Seconds/state, single core | Evidence, classification and approach |
|---|---:|---|
| Surface/maps | 39.87 CPU | Atom pairs and spatially indexed accessible points; repeated local queries and mutation. **(b/c, provisional)** Favor compact spatial lists and fused or native kernels; quantify bytes before claiming bandwidth saturation. |
| Solver | 8.54 CPU | Red/black SOR, early convergence; **(c)**. Use fused in-place native stencil or tiled XLA with identical rounding/stops. |
| Energy | 0.12 CPU | Surface reaction-field and charge sums; **(a)** repetitive reductions, suitable for JAX; little end-to-end headroom. |
| Setup, boundaries, potentials, Python/other PB | Not isolated | Coulomb/focusing boundaries and site interpolation; **(a/c), unconfirmed**. Measure before optimizing. |

The table's internal intervals are **CPU time, not wall time**: DelPhi logs bracket map elaboration, iterations and energy, but do not expose independent stage wall timers. They exclude portions of setup and postprocessing and must not be subtracted from the uninstrumented PB wall. Whole-PB wall and per-call instrumented wall are in the JSON.

The five states contain 2,550 solves: 510/state, 30,870 sweeps/state (10–100/solve), or **16.41 billion gridpoint-sweeps/state**. Maximum potential change is checked every 10 sweeps against 0.01 kT/e, with a 500-sweep cap; all stopped below threshold (largest 0.009997). This is not an equation residual. The earlier five-state jaxpb profile recorded 510 useful solves/state and 31,280 useful sweeps/state (16.62 billion gridpoint-sweeps), but 85,056 executed lane-sweeps/state including padding and finished lanes. That older profile used different lane widths from OPT4. Thus similar useful solver work, not fewer grids or a looser stopping rule, explains little of the gap; padding and implementation are candidates. Atom-query counts are unavailable for an equal map-work comparison.

One float32 grid is 2.03 MiB, below the 32 MiB L3 per CCD, but the full working set and actual traffic are unknown. Even an idealized potential read plus write is 8 bytes/point/sweep; neighbor and coefficient traffic add to this. The installed binary has symbols but no available Fortran source; compute-node `ltrace` was absent and hardware counters were not measured. In-place layout, arithmetic intensity, cache behavior and compute versus memory limits therefore remain unverified. As an **optimistic estimate**, eliminating OPT4 maps entirely caps overall speedup at 1.91×; eliminating SOR caps it at 1.85×. OPT4's separately measured native-stencil prototype suggests about 202 s/state if rounding and integration costs can be solved; it is not a validated production result.
