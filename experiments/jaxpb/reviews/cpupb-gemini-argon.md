### 2. SOR (`163.81 s/state` on 5-state CPU: `sor1 96.95 s`, `sor2 66.86 s`)

#### What Limits the XLA-CPU Stencil ([sor.py:130-163](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/sor.py#L130-L163), [sor.py:209-270](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/sor.py#L209-L270))

With `CPU_LANE_WIDTHS = (4, 8, 16)` ([records.py:149](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/records.py#L149)) and device compaction ([sor.py:240-270](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/sor.py#L240-L270)), lane occupancy is already **92.26%** (`156,400` useful vs `169,520` executed lane-sweeps across 5 states; lockstep waste is only **1.084×**, [opt2.json:574-580](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/results/opt2.json#L574-L580)). The limit is the per-point execution cost: **`9.09 ns` per executed sweep-point** (`191 sweeps/s`), driven by:
1. **Unfused `jnp.roll` and coefficient gathers spill L3 cache by 4.5×–18× (`36.1 MB/lane` temp):**
   In [opt2.json:6713-6736](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/results/opt2.json#L6713-L6736), XLA-CPU compiles `device_solve` with **`578.2 MB` temp at $W=16$**, **`289.1 MB` at $W=8$**, and **`144.6 MB` at $W=4$** ($17 \times 81^3 \times 4\text{ B} = 36.14\text{ MB/lane}$). XLA-CPU materializes the gathered 4×f32 operator fields `exE[op], eyE[op], ezE[op], dvE[op]` ($8.50\text{ MB/lane}$, [sor.py:157](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/sor.py#L157)), `p[ix]` and `rhs[ix]` copies in `device_solve` ([sor.py:257](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/sor.py#L257)), and separate `slice`+`concat` buffers for the **9 `jnp.roll` calls per half-sweep** (6 on `p`, 3 on `ex, ey, ez`, [sor.py:133-135](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/sor.py#L133-L135)). Even at $W=4$, 144.6 MB is **4.5× larger than the 32 MB L3 cache**, forcing every half-sweep to stream from DRAM at ~12–14.5 GB/s.
2. **2.16× wasted full-grid work per half-sweep:**
   `sor_chunk` ([sor.py:132-141](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/sor.py#L132-L141)) evaluates `gs(p)` on all $81^3 = 531,441$ points (both colours + 38,402 face points) twice per sweep and masks out 53.6% via `jnp.where(cA/cB, ...)` (only $79^3 / 2 = 246,520$ interior points update per half-sweep).

#### Is a Bitwise-Preserving CPU Speed-up Possible?

**Yes.** For each interior point $(i,j,k)$ of active parity, the exact IEEE-754 float32 update in [sor.py:132-141](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/sor.py#L132-L141) is:
$$\text{nb} = (((((e_{x,i,j,k} p_{i+1,j,k} + e_{x,i-1,j,k} p_{i-1,j,k}) + e_{y,i,j,k} p_{i,j+1,k}) + e_{y,i,j-1,k} p_{i,j-1,k}) + e_{z,i,j,k} p_{i,j,k+1}) + e_{z,i,j,k-1} p_{i,j,k-1})$$
$$\text{gs} = (\text{nb} + \text{rhs}_{i,j,k}) \cdot \text{dinv}_{i,j,k}, \qquad p^{\text{new}}_{i,j,k} = p_{i,j,k} + \omega \cdot (\text{gs} - p_{i,j,k})$$
Phase 2's CPU packed-colour trial (`s1d`, [opt2.json:7686-7896](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/results/opt2.json#L7686-L7896)) and split-check trial (`s2`, [opt2.json:7899-7940](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/results/opt2.json#L7899-L7940)) changed rounding *only* because reshaping $81 \to 265,721$ or unrolling the 10th sweep changed how XLA-CPU's LLVM pass contracted `a*b + c` into FMA vs discrete multiply+add and peeled vector loop tails. Conversely, changing outer lane widths (`tune-cpu`, [opt2.json:5751-5895](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/results/opt2.json#L5751-L5895)) was **100% bitwise identical** because the inner $(81,81,81)$ axes and loop body were untouched.

### 3. Geometry (`44.35 s/state` whole-molecule + `3.69 s/state` model compounds = `48.04 s/state`)

#### What Is Slow (with `file:line`)
1. **54 per-point `jnp.searchsorted` binary searches in `_cand` ([geometry.py:113-122](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/geometry.py#L113-L122)):**

2. **432-slot padded `float64` `_burial` at `GEO_CHUNK = 131,072` ([geometry.py:87-97](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/geometry.py#L87-L97), [records.py:144-145](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/records.py#L144-L145)):**
   Each chunk evaluates `131,072 × 27 × 16 = 56.6M` 3D `float64` `sqrt` distances (`57.2%` of `geo_probes`), materializing `cpos[ii]` (`1.36 GB`), `dd` (`0.45 GB`), and `cand` (`0.45 GB` int64) = **`2.30 GB` compiled XLA-CPU temp** ([opt2.json:5928](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/results/opt2.json#L5928), **72× L3 cache**). Yet across 27 cells of size $\sim 3.4\text{ \AA}$, there are only ~20–35 real atoms on average (max single-cell occupancy across all 104 L3 states is `11`, [speed.json:28](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/results/speed.json#L28)), so **>90% of the 432 slots are `-1` padding**.
3. **Uncompacted model-compound `_site_probes` ([geometry.py:442-475](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/geometry.py#L442-L475)):**
   Expands all $\binom{32}{2} \times 720 + 32 \times 1500 = 405,120$ candidate points per site (>92% invalid padding!) and vmaps `_burial` across all $B=16$ sites at once, allocating **`8.52 GB` compiled XLA-CPU temp** (`9.66 GB` warm HWM, [opt2.json:5938,6192](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/results/opt2.json#L5938)) — **the #1 memory bottleneck in the entire CPU pipeline**.

#### What Can Change Without Changing Outputs (100% Bitwise Identical)
Look at [_burial](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/geometry.py#L87-L97):
- For each candidate atom $a = \text{cand}[q, s]$, `dd` is a scalar function of $(pt_q, \text{cpos}_a)$ only.
- The three reductions across the candidate axis (`axis=1`) are `dmin_in = jnp.min(...)` (IEEE-754 `min`), `rank = jnp.sum(v & (dd < dmin_in[:, None]), axis=1)` (integer boolean count), and `anyin = jnp.any(inside, axis=1)` (boolean OR).
- **All three reductions are strictly associative, commutative, and invariant to the number of `-1` padding slots and candidate slot order!** And [_probe_loops](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/geometry.py#L226-L245) compacts each chunk via `jnp.nonzero(keep)` and writes to `buf` and `kidx` at the running offset `off`, which is **invariant to the chunk size `CH`**.
- Therefore, the following changes are **mathematically guaranteed to be 100% bitwise identical** (same `probes`, `kidx`, `n_probes`, `geo_cell_occ`, `knn_truncation_changed`):
  1. **Direct $O(1)$ 3D cell-table lookup instead of 54 `searchsorted` calls ([geometry.py:100-122](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/geometry.py#L100-L122)):**
     A protein in classes 0–4 spans at most $<128$ cells of $3.4\text{ \AA}$ ($128 \times 3.4 = 435\text{ \AA}$). Setting `NCV = 128` (or `64` for c0–c2, `128` for c3–c4) lets `_cell_hash` build `bounds = jnp.searchsorted(scid, jnp.arange(NCV**3 + 1), side="left").astype(jnp.int32)` once per state ($64^3 \times 4\text{ B} = 1\text{ MB}$), and `_cand` replaces `2 × 27` binary searches per point with direct indexing `st = bounds[cid]; en = bounds[cid + 1]`.
  2. **Per-atom / per-pair (or 27-cell compacted) candidate lists of width $K_{27} = 96\text{–}128$ in `int32` ([geometry.py:113-122,162-177](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/geometry.py#L113-L122)):**
     Compact the 27 neighbour cells' atoms into a single list of width $K_{27} = 128$ (via a 27-element prefix sum on `en - st`, or once per occupied cell/atom since all 1,500 vertices of atom $a$ lie within $2\text{ cells}$ of `pos[a]`, with automatic retry at $432$ if `max(tot) > 128`). Cuts `_burial` `float64` distance work and gathers by **3.4×** (`432 -> 128`).
  3. **Shrink CPU `CH` from `131,072` to `8,192` and cast `order`/`cand` to `int32` ([geometry.py:109-122,249-263](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/geometry.py#L249-L263)):**
     Drops `geo_probes` CPU temp from **`2.30 GB` to `~45 MB`** (L3-resident).
  4. **Group/chunk `geometry_sites` ([geometry.py:442-475](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/geometry.py#L442-L475)):**
     Run `_site_probes` via `jax.lax.map` over single sites (or groups of 2) and chunk `_burial` over the 405,120 candidate points in tiles of `4,096` (or compact `valid` points first): drops CPU temp from **`8.52 GB` to `<0.05 GB`** and time from `3.69 s` to `~0.4 s/state`.

#### Three Structural Reasons XLA-CPU Intra-Op Threading Wastes the 12-CPU Allocation in PB
1. **Width-4 `vmap` bottleneck on 6 physical cores / 12 threads:** In `maps_batch` (`group = 4`, [maps.py:603](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/maps.py#L603)), XLA-CPU parallelizes per-site operations (`argsort`, `searchsorted`, `nonzero` 11×/site) across the outer batch axis of size **4**. Four tasks cannot fill 6 physical cores (12 threads)—2 physical cores sit idle during every per-site sort and compaction.
2. **Tens of thousands of fine-grained Eigen fork/join barriers:** `_dyn_rows` (`chunk=269`/`58`/`50`), `reach_mask_b` (`ch=123`), and `sor_chunk` execute **>50,000 HLO loop iterations per state**, synchronizing the 12-thread Eigen pool on every tile/half-sweep.
3. **Collective cache thrashing:** When 12 threads cooperate on one $W=16$ SOR block (`578 MB` temp) or one `geo_probes` chunk (`2.30 GB` temp), they share a working set 18×–72× larger than the 32 MB L3 cache.
4. **How to fix thread utilization (within-state and across-state):**
   - **Within a state (latency + throughput):** Mirror `mc_cpu` ([titrate.py:321-352](file:///Users/alexi/Documents/Codex/2026-10-10/can-x20/work/jaxpb-opt/src/jaxpb/titrate.py#L321-L352)) — compile **single-site (`B=1`) maps** and **single-lane (`W=1`) SOR** executables (or C callbacks) whose per-worker working sets (<15–23 MB) fit inside L2/L3, and dispatch the state's 16–80 sites and 50–200 tautomer lanes across 6–12 host `ThreadPoolExecutor` workers (JAX releases the GIL inside XLA calls).
   - **Process-level parallelism across states:** Today, 10.57 GB peak RSS prevents running multiple processes in a 24 GB (`12 × 2 GB`) job. Once `geometry_sites` (`8.52 GB` $\to$ `<0.05 GB`) and `geo_probes` (`2.30 GB` $\to$ `<0.05 GB`) are fixed, per-process RSS drops to **~1.5–2.2 GB**, allowing **3–6 worker processes (2–4 threads each)** across independent states/triples in batch runs for near-linear core scaling.
